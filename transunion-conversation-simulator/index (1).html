How to Test Drive the Call

The assistant expects you to say whether you are adding or removing a freeze. It records what you say and moves on — it will not read your request back to you, so state it clearly on your first turn.

To add a freeze

  • “I want to put a freeze on my credit.”
  • “I need to freeze my credit report.”
  • “Can you place a security freeze on my file?”
  • “Someone opened an account in my name and I want my credit locked down.”

To remove a freeze

  • “I need to remove the freeze on my credit.”
  • “I want to lift my credit freeze — I'm applying for a car loan.”
  • “Please unfreeze my credit report.”
  • “Can you take the freeze off my file?”

What the call does

Six stages, in fixed order. Identity details are collected in two grouped rounds rather than one field at a time, and each round is read back for confirmation before it is stored.

  1. Your request is captured

    The assistant logs your request as add or remove and moves straight on. If you never said which one, it asks you once.

  2. What a freeze does, and your choice to continue

    You hear what a freeze protects and that you can also do this yourself at transunion.com/creditfreeze. Then you are asked whether to continue by phone.

    Say yes and identity collection begins. Say no and the call ends there — worth running once to see the self-service handoff.

  3. Identity, first round

    Mailing ZIP code and the last four digits of your Social Security number, asked together and confirmed together.

    Ask it to try again and it re-asks both values as a pair. Give one wrong value at confirmation and it re-asks them one at a time.

  4. Identity, second round

    Date of birth and the numeric part of your street address, again asked and confirmed together.

    Say the date however you like — “October tenth nineteen seventy six” or “ten ten seventy six” both work. The assistant should not dictate a format or reject a spoken date.

  5. Verification runs

    The check happens silently. You will not hear a pass or fail announcement, and you will not be asked for any of the details a second time.

  6. Outcome

    One of three paths, chosen by the verification result and your original request.

    Match, add

    The freeze is placed and confirmed back to you.

    Call ends
    Match, remove

    The freeze is removed and confirmed back to you.

    Call ends
    No match

    Any value outside the two records. You hear that the account can't be found, plus a pointer to the online option.

    Transferred to a live agent

Questions you can ask

These are answered from the assistant's own content. Ask them at any point in the call — it should answer, then pick the flow back up where it left off.

What is a credit freeze?
Expect: a freeze stops your credit from being pulled for inquiries or used to open new accounts while it is in place, and you will need to lift it before applying for credit.
Is a freeze permanent?
Expect: you control how long it lasts — the report stays frozen until you ask for the freeze to be removed.
How long does it take for a freeze to go into effect?
Expect: placing one by phone or online takes effect almost immediately, and a temporary lift can too — though waiting a few minutes before a lender pulls the report is advised.
Can I lift the freeze temporarily instead of removing it?
Expect: the temporary lift is covered in the assistant's answers and pointed toward phone or online self-service.

Questions that should escalate

The assistant handles credit freezes and nothing else. Ask one of these to see where the boundary sits: credit questions it has no answer for hand off to an agent, while unrelated topics get a scope response.

  • “I want to dispute something on my report.”
  • “Can you mail me a copy of my credit report?”
  • “Why did my score drop 40 points?”
  • “I need to cancel my subscription.”
  • “Do I have to freeze my credit at Experian too?”
  • “How do I add a fraud alert?”

Behaviour to test deliberately

These rules sit above the six stages and override them whenever they are triggered.

Asking for a person, early
Your first request during stages 1 and 2 is deflected toward self-service. Ask a second time and you are transferred.
Asking for a person, mid-flow
From stage 3 onward, the first request transfers you immediately. No self-service pitch, no attempt to finish verification, and whatever was collected is abandoned.
Mumbling or going quiet
After two unclear turns the assistant says it's having trouble understanding and transfers you.
Declining to continue by phone
Say no at stage 2 and the call closes after the online pointer, without collecting anything.
Changing your mind mid-call
Try switching from add to remove partway through to see how the flow holds its captured request.

What the voice demo won't do

  • Change anything on a real credit file
  • Handle disputes, credit reports, billing, or score questions
  • Connect you to a live human — transfers are attempted, not completed
  • Take input through the keypad
  • Place a temporary lift for a set date range
  • Cover the other two credit bureaus

How to Test Drive the Chat

The first assistant listens for fraud, alerts, or identity theft. It won't ask you to confirm exactly what you want — it acknowledges the concern and hands you into the fraud alert flow. Describe the problem in your own words, or name the task directly.

Describing the problem

  • “My identity was stolen — what can I do to protect my credit?”
  • “I think someone opened a credit card in my name.”
  • “My wallet was stolen and I'm worried about fraud.”
  • “I got a letter about an application I never made.”

Naming the task

  • “How do I add a fraud alert to my credit file?”
  • “I want to place a fraud alert.”
  • “I need to remove a fraud alert.”
  • “Can you take the fraud alert off my report?”

Ask your questions first. Once the handoff happens, the fraud alert flow is button-and-form driven and won't take free-text questions. The question bank below belongs in your opening turns.

What the chat does

An open conversation for the first two stages, then a deterministic flow. Add and remove follow the same shape, with one extra screen on the add path.

  1. You describe the problem

    The assistant acknowledges what you said before doing anything else. This is where free-text questions get answered.

  2. Handoff into the fraud alert flow

    A system transfer moves you out of the open conversation and into the guided flow. You'll see a short greeting and a prompt to confirm what you need.

  3. Choose your task

    Two buttons: Add a Fraud Alert or Remove Fraud Alert. Everything after this point follows your choice.

    The add path also explains that TransUnion offers three types of fraud alert before asking you to sign in.

  4. Sign in

    Log in or create an account. Both are simulated — choose Log in to keep moving.

  5. Verify your identity

    A form appears in the chat window asking for ZIP code, Social Security number, date of birth, and the numeric part of your street address.

    Any values work here. The chat demo doesn't check them against a record, so there is no failure path to test — put in whatever you like. The date of birth field is labelled DD/MM/YYYY, day first.

  6. Confirm the action

    Select Add Initial Fraud Alert or Remove Alert to commit.

    On the add path only, a second form offers to collect phone numbers so lenders can call you to verify applications. It's optional — submit the empty form to skip it.

  7. Confirmation and close

    The flow confirms the alert was added or removed, then asks whether the virtual assistant resolved your issue.

    Alert added

    An initial fraud alert is confirmed on the account.

    Survey, then thank you
    Alert removed

    The alert is confirmed as removed from the account.

    Survey, then thank you
    Either answer

    Yes and no both reach the same closing message in this demo.

    Flow ends

    To run it again: end the chat from the menu in the top right corner of the window, then reopen the link. That resets the simulator.

Questions you can ask

Ask these in your opening turns, before the handoff. They're the questions a worried customer actually asks when deciding between an alert and a freeze.

When should I use a fraud alert?
Expect: when you want lenders to take an extra step and call to verify applications in your name, without blocking access to your credit report entirely.
How fast is it?
Expect: the alert appears on your credit report the same day you place it.
How does it protect me?
Expect: lenders call to verify credit applications made in your name, which helps you spot identity theft.
How long does it last?
Expect: an initial fraud alert lasts one year, and is a good option if you're worried about becoming a victim of fraud or identity theft.

Questions that should close the chat

This assistant covers fraud alerts only. Credit questions it has no answer for are not guessed at — it says so and ends the conversation rather than transferring. Unrelated topics get a scope response.

  • “What's my credit score?”
  • “I want to dispute a late payment.”
  • “How do I freeze my credit instead?”
  • “Can I get my report mailed to me?”
  • “Who ran a hard inquiry on my file?”
  • “I need help with my bill.”

Behaviour to test deliberately

Vague first message
Open with something like “I have a problem” and see whether it draws out the fraud concern before handing off.
Emotional framing
Lead with the worry rather than the task. The assistant is meant to acknowledge the concern before starting anything.
Two unclear turns
Send two messages it can't interpret. It should close the conversation rather than keep guessing.
Asking for a person
There is no live agent transfer in the chat demo — worth seeing how the request is handled.
Abandoning mid-form
Leave the verification form and try typing a message instead to see how the guided flow responds.
